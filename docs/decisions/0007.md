# ADR-0007: Multiple-testing correction
Date: 2026-05-18
Status: Proposed — awaiting Armin sign-off

## Context

ADR-0006 runs pseudobulk DE per cell type. Each run produces p-values
for ~15,000 expressed genes. With (say) eight cell types in the
substantia nigra, that is 8 × 15,000 = 120,000 tests in total. Without
multiple-testing correction, false discoveries dominate.

Two correction regimes are reasonable, and they answer different
questions:

- **Per-cell-type FDR** answers "of the genes called significant *within
  this cell type*, what fraction are false discoveries?"
- **Global FDR** answers "of the genes called significant *anywhere in
  any cell type*, what fraction are false discoveries?"

The choice has real consequences for which genes the dossier highlights.
Per-cell-type FDR is more permissive and is the standard reporting
convention in cell-type-conditioned DE; global FDR is more conservative
and is what a hostile statistical reviewer might want to see.

## Options considered

### A. Per-cell-type BH-FDR at α = 0.05
Apply Benjamini-Hochberg within each cell type's gene-level p-value
vector. Report significance per cell type.
- Pros: matches the standard reporting convention; preserves the
  cell-type-conditioned framing the rest of the pipeline already uses;
  allows a gene to be "significant in DA neurons" without dragging in
  unrelated cell types' multiple-testing burden.
- Cons: more permissive overall; harder to argue when ranking across
  cell types in the dossier's composite view.

### B. Global BH-FDR at α = 0.05
Apply Benjamini-Hochberg across the full pooled p-value vector spanning
all cell types × genes.
- Pros: most conservative; strongest claim ("significant globally").
- Cons: under-powered when most cell types are quiet; can mask
  cell-type-specific PD signals that would have been clear in
  cell-type-level correction.

### C. Hierarchical FDR
Stage 1: select cell types where the gene-level p-value distribution is
non-uniform. Stage 2: BH-FDR within those cell types only.
- Pros: principled compromise.
- Cons: more code; harder to explain in the README; less commonly seen
  in this literature.

## Criteria for choosing

1. **Match to the question dossiers are answering.** Dossiers report
   per-cell-type expression and PD-vs-control effect within those cell
   types. The natural correction matches the framing.
2. **Defensibility under hostile review.** A reviewer who insists on
   global FDR must be able to see that number too.
3. **No silent choice.** Whichever is primary, the dossier must say so;
   whichever is secondary, the dossier must also report it.

## Decision

**Primary: Option A.** Per-cell-type Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at α = 0.05,
applied within each cell type's p-value vector independently.

**Secondary (reported alongside): Option B.** Global BH-FDR is also
computed across the pooled p-value vector and reported in the dossier's
"detail" view (collapsible section) and in the per-gene CSV export.

A gene that is significant under both per-cell-type FDR and global FDR
gets a "strong evidence" annotation in the composite score (ADR-0008).

## Consequences

- The dossier's headline DE volcano (per cell type) uses per-cell-type
  FDR coloring.
- The dossier's per-gene table shows both `padj_within_celltype` and
  `padj_global` columns.
- The README's "Method" section must explicitly state which FDR is
  the primary; failing to do so is a frequent source of legitimate
  reviewer criticism.

## Open questions for Armin

1. Confirm α = 0.05, or override (some target-credentialing teams use
   0.10 to widen the funnel of hypotheses; defensible either way).
2. Confirm reporting both per-cell-type and global FDR, or just one.
