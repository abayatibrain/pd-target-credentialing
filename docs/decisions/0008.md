# ADR-0008: OpenTargets evidence weighting and composite score formula
Date: 2026-05-18
Status: Proposed — awaiting Armin sign-off
Depends on: ADR-0009 (calibration). If ADR-0009's calibration check
fails, this ADR is superseded.

## Context

The dossier's headline number is a composite confidence score that
combines disease-specific transcriptional evidence (from ADR-0006's
pseudobulk DE) with disease-association evidence from OpenTargets. The
score must (a) be defensible to a hostile reviewer, (b) reproduce
expected rankings for well-known PD genes, and (c) **not** create the
illusion of precision where the underlying evidence is sparse.

OpenTargets aggregates target-disease evidence across many datatypes
(genetic association, somatic mutation, drugs, literature, animal models,
expression in disease tissue, RNA expression, pathways). For
neurodegenerative diseases, the highest-information channels are
typically: genetic association, literature, and animal models. Other
channels exist and may carry signal, but with different reliability
characteristics.

This ADR commits to a specific composite formula and weights. ADR-0009
is the calibration check that will either validate or invalidate these
choices using anchor genes.

## Options considered

### A. Equal-weight average of all OT datatypes + DE
Average every available OT datatype score and add a normalized DE term.
- Pros: no subjective weighting.
- Cons: literally false equality between unequal evidence channels;
  treats animal-model evidence as informative as genetic evidence, which
  reverses the field's established hierarchy.

### B. Hierarchy-weighted OT score + sign-aware DE bonus (proposed default)
Weight OT evidence types in line with the field's standard hierarchy:
genetic > literature > animal. Combine with a bounded contribution from
this repo's PD-cell-type DE result.
- Pros: reflects evidence-quality conventions; bounded DE contribution
  prevents a single result here from dominating decades of human-genetics
  evidence; calibration-testable.
- Cons: weights are subjective surface and must be defended (this ADR)
  and calibrated (ADR-0009).

### C. Use OpenTargets' built-in association score directly, no DE bonus
Just display the OT score; no transcriptional component.
- Pros: simplest, fully outsourced to OT.
- Cons: throws away the most distinctive thing this repo computes (the
  per-cell-type PD vs control DE) and reduces the repo to an OT
  visualizer.

## Criteria

1. **Hierarchy of evidence respected.** Human-genetics evidence is
   load-bearing for therapeutic target credentialing in a way that animal
   models are not.
2. **DE evidence visible but bounded.** The pipeline's distinctive output
   should contribute but not dominate.
3. **Sign-aware.** A gene that is upregulated in PD vs control DA neurons
   does *not* automatically deserve higher confidence than a downregulated
   one — both are biologically meaningful — but a near-zero DE result
   should not get a bonus.
4. **Calibratable.** Anchor genes (ADR-0009) must rank as expected.

## Decision

**Option B.** Composite score per gene `g` for disease `d` is:

```
S(g, d) = w_g · OT_genetic(g, d)
       + w_l · OT_literature(g, d)
       + w_a · OT_animal(g, d)
       + bonus(DE_PD_vs_control(g, DA_neuron))
```

Where:
- All OT terms are normalized to [0, 1] using OpenTargets' own
  normalization (the per-datatype scores returned by the platform).
- `w_g = 0.5`, `w_l = 0.3`, `w_a = 0.2`. These weights sum to 1; the
  base score (without bonus) is therefore in [0, 1].
- `bonus(...)` is computed from the DA-neuron pseudobulk DE result for
  the gene:
  - `magnitude = min(|log2FC| / 1.5, 1.0)` (saturates above |log2FC| = 1.5)
  - `passes_fdr = 1 if padj_within_celltype < 0.05 else 0` (per ADR-0007)
  - `bonus = 0.15 · magnitude · passes_fdr`
- Final score `S` is clipped to [0, 1]. Confidence interval comes from
  bootstrap over evidence-subsampling (n = 1000) plus per-LFC bootstrap
  variance from ADR-0006.

A gene with no available OT evidence in any of the three channels gets a
score of 0 and a "no evidence" flag in the dossier (not "low score" —
they are different states and the dossier must distinguish them).

A gene with strong DE but zero OT evidence cannot exceed 0.15 by the
formula above — this is deliberate. The DE result here is one cohort,
one cell type, one direction of effect. It is not a substitute for
human-genetics evidence.

## Consequences

- The weights `(0.5, 0.3, 0.2)` are committed in code (`config.py`) and
  documented in the dossier's score-breakdown panel; users can audit the
  contribution of each term.
- The 0.15 DE bonus cap is the single value most likely to need
  recalibration when ADR-0009's anchor check runs. ADR-0009 supersedes
  this ADR if calibration fails.
- The composite score is **not** a probability of any clinical or
  preclinical event. The dossier copy must not say "P(success) = 0.84"
  or anything similarly mis-readable. The score is "evidence-convergence
  index, calibrated against anchor genes."

## Open questions for Armin

1. Confirm the weight triple `(0.5, 0.3, 0.2)` for genetic / literature /
   animal, or override.
2. Confirm the DE-bonus cap of 0.15 and the |log2FC| = 1.5 saturation
   threshold, or override.
3. Should drug-evidence and pathway-evidence channels from OT be added
   to the formula, or is the genetic / literature / animal triad
   sufficient for v1.0.0?
4. Confirm "score is evidence-convergence index, not probability of
   anything." This wording will appear in every dossier; precise phrasing
   is your call.
